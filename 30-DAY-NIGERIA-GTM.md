# YayaAiki 30-Day Nigeria Go-to-Market Plan

**Objective:** Secure **3–5 paying anchor customers** for a Nigeria-first Work Assurance pilot in 30 days.  
**Operating model:** Self-service web front door, human-assisted fulfillment behind the interface, licensed payment execution, one authoritative work record.  
**North Star:** **Repeat Assured Work** — a real buyer creates a real work order, a professional delivers, evidence is reviewed, the buyer accepts, payment is confirmed, and the same buyer orders again.

## The offer to sell

Do not sell “access to African freelancers.” Sell a measurable business result:

> **YayaAiki gives Nigerian businesses a structured way to commission repeatable work, define acceptance before execution, receive evidence, review or request rework, and keep a downloadable Work Assurance Record after payment.**

The pilot offer is deliberately narrow:

- one buyer organization;
- one repeatable work family selected from data QA, transcription, translation, research, catalogue QA, or document/data processing;
- one clearly defined acceptance standard;
- one assigned professional or small managed pool;
- one buyer reviewer and one independent YayaAiki reviewer;
- one NGN payment flow through a licensed provider;
- one assurance record per accepted work order.

The customer is buying **reliable, auditable completion**, not a directory listing.

## Ideal customer profile

Prioritize organizations already buying work in batches at least weekly or monthly. Avoid customers with one-off vague projects during the first 30 days.

### Primary segment: Nigerian organizations with repeat operational work

Target Lagos and Abuja first, with remote delivery across Nigeria. Build a list of at least 80 organizations across the following segments:

| Segment | Trigger | Example pilot work | Why it can buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI/data companies | Large labelled or reviewed datasets | classification, evaluation, QA | repeat volume and measurable acceptance |
| Research agencies | recurring studies and interviews | transcription, coding, verification | evidence and audit trail matter |
| E-commerce and retail | catalogue expansion or cleanup | product-data QA, images, inventory checks | objective criteria and recurring batches |
| Agencies and BPOs | overflow or client deadlines | QA, content operations, structured processing | can resell capacity and repeat |
| NGOs and public-interest programs | funded field/data programs | structured collection and validation | need traceability and reporting |
| Enterprise operations teams | backlogs and document processing | data cleanup, review, reconciliation | clear cost of delay and error |

### Buyer personas

Contact the person who owns the pain and budget, not only founders. Prioritize heads of operations, research directors, data/AI operations leads, customer operations leads, procurement managers, and agency owners.

### Disqualification criteria

Do not spend pilot time on prospects that have no recurring work, cannot define acceptance, expect unpaid proof of concept, require YayaAiki to hold funds, demand a pan-African rollout immediately, or refuse a paid first batch.

## Pricing hypothesis

Pricing is a hypothesis to test, not a public permanent tariff. Quote in NGN and separate professional payout from YayaAiki fees.

### Pilot structure

- **Paid discovery and setup:** NGN 150,000–300,000 depending on workflow complexity. Credit this against the first production batch if the customer proceeds.
- **Per accepted work order or batch:** direct professional payout plus a **20–30% YayaAiki gross service margin target** before payment and exceptional support costs.
- **Verification-intensive work:** add a fixed QA/assurance fee or price per accepted unit, not only per hour.
- **Minimum pilot commitment:** NGN 500,000–1,500,000 over the first 30–45 days, or an equivalent signed commitment with a defined first batch.

Do not promise a final price before reviewing the sample task and acceptance standard. Quote a range, confirm the buyer’s budget, and state what is included: scope design, assignment, evidence collection, review, rework handling, payment status, and assurance record.

### Economic guardrail

For every pilot, calculate:

`buyer revenue – professional payout – reviewer cost – payment fees – fraud/dispute reserve – support cost – acquisition cost = contribution margin`

Do not accept work that cannot plausibly achieve positive contribution margin after the first learning cycle. Report review minutes and rework, not only revenue.

## The sales funnel and weekly targets

The 30-day target is not 500 vague leads. It is a controlled funnel.

| Funnel stage | 30-day target |
|---|---:|
| Named accounts researched | 80 |
| Personalized first contacts | 60 |
| Positive replies or referrals | 20 |
| Discovery calls completed | 12 |
| Paid workflow design sessions | 6 |
| Pilot proposals sent | 6 |
| Paid anchor customers | 3–5 |
| First paid work orders | 3–5 |

## Day-by-day execution plan

### Days 1–2: lock the sales asset and proof language

Create a one-page pilot brief, a two-minute product walkthrough, a sample Work Assurance Record, a sample acceptance-criteria checklist, and a one-page security/data-handling summary.

Use this headline:

> **Turn recurring operational work into an accepted, auditable record.**

Use this supporting statement:

> **YayaAiki helps your team define the work, assign capacity, collect evidence, verify delivery, manage rework, confirm payment, and download the record. You can start with one paid batch; no long implementation is required.**

Label every example as illustrative. Do not claim customers, volume, payment partners, quality rates, or “first in Africa” status without evidence.

**Deliverables:** landing-page CTA “Start a paid pilot,” pilot brief, discovery script, qualification form, proposal template, and CRM.

### Days 3–5: build the account list and warm-introduction map

Research 80 target organizations. Score each from 1–5 on recurring work, measurable acceptance, budget access, urgency, and referral proximity. Select the top 30 accounts for personalized outreach and 10 for warm introductions.

Sources for introductions include operators, research consultants, agency owners, accelerators, startup communities, data vendors, university innovation hubs, professional associations, and existing business contacts.

**Exit criteria:** 60 named contacts, 10 warm-intro requests, and 30 personalized messages ready.

### Days 6–8: launch the first outbound sequence

Send no more than 15 high-quality first messages per day. Each message should mention a specific operational trigger and ask for a 20-minute workflow conversation, not a generic product demo.

#### Initial message

> **Subject: Making [specific recurring workflow] easier to accept and audit**
>
> Hi [Name], I’m building YayaAiki for teams that repeatedly buy or coordinate [workflow]. The pilot turns each batch into a clear brief with acceptance criteria, evidence, review/rework, payment status, and a downloadable completion record. I’m looking for 3–5 Nigerian teams to run one paid batch in the next 30 days. Are you open to a 20-minute conversation about where [workflow] currently breaks down?

#### Follow-up after two business days

> Hi [Name], following up because teams doing [workflow] often lose time on unclear briefs, rework, missing proof, and payment reconciliation. If you have a recurring batch in the next month, I can map the workflow and return a fixed pilot scope and price. Would [two specific time options] work?

#### Referral request

> I’m looking for one operations or data lead who buys repeatable batches of work and cares about measurable acceptance. Who in your network owns that problem?

### Days 9–12: discovery calls and workflow qualification

Run 12 discovery calls. Do not pitch a broad platform. Diagnose the workflow using these questions:

1. What repeatable work do you buy or coordinate each month?
2. How many units or batches are typical?
3. What exactly makes a delivery acceptable?
4. Who currently performs and reviews the work?
5. What is the cost of rework, error, delay, or failed delivery?
6. How is evidence captured today?
7. How are disputes and payment approvals handled?
8. What data sensitivity or client confidentiality applies?
9. What is the budget and approval path for a first paid batch?
10. What result would make you order the next batch?

Score each prospect:

- **A:** recurring volume, clear criteria, decision-maker present, budget, urgent pain;
- **B:** good pain but unclear criteria or buyer access;
- **C:** curiosity without volume, budget, or timeline.

Only A prospects receive a pilot proposal.

### Days 13–15: convert calls into paid workflow design sessions

Offer six paid workflow-design sessions. The session produces a concrete pilot specification, not a free consulting report.

The output includes task definition, acceptance criteria, evidence requirements, review method, estimated capacity, price range, timeline, data class, and success metrics.

A design session is paid because it creates reusable value and prevents free custom-scoping from consuming the team. Credit the fee against the first production batch when appropriate.

### Days 16–18: send proposals and close the first two anchors

Send proposals within 24 hours of a qualified session. Each proposal should include:

- business problem;
- first workflow and unit definition;
- scope and exclusions;
- acceptance criteria;
- evidence pack requirements;
- professional and reviewer responsibilities;
- timeline;
- NGN price and payment schedule;
- data handling and confidentiality assumptions;
- rework and dispute process;
- success metrics;
- pilot-to-repeat conversion terms;
- explicit statement that payment execution remains with a licensed provider.

Ask for a signed pilot order and deposit or provider-confirmed funding before starting work. Do not begin an unbounded free trial.

### Days 19–21: deliver the first paid batch manually but rigorously

Use the current web product as the front door, while operations handles exceptions manually. Every paid batch must produce:

- a work-order identifier;
- versioned acceptance criteria;
- assignment record;
- evidence pack;
- reviewer decision;
- rework history if applicable;
- buyer acceptance;
- payment status;
- assurance record;
- contribution-margin calculation.

Ask the buyer for a 15-minute acceptance review immediately after delivery. Record the exact reason for acceptance, rework, or rejection.

### Days 22–24: use proof to close anchors three through five

Turn the first accepted work into an anonymized case study only with buyer permission. Show the workflow and measured outcome, not inflated marketing claims.

Example case-study structure:

> A Nigerian [segment] used YayaAiki for [workflow]. The batch contained [number] units, used [number] acceptance criteria, required [review time], resulted in [acceptance/rework result], and produced a structured assurance record. The buyer’s next batch is scheduled for [date] or is under written consideration.

Use this proof in follow-ups to the remaining A prospects. Ask for a specific next step: paid pilot, paid design session, or referral.

### Days 25–27: secure repeat commitments

For every pilot customer, schedule a post-delivery review. Ask:

- What was more reliable than your prior process?
- What was slower or harder?
- Which evidence would your finance, operations, or client team reuse?
- What would make you send the next batch through YayaAiki?
- What monthly volume and budget could you commit to?

Close a second-order commitment, monthly minimum, or defined next batch. The first order proves willingness to try; the second proves economic value.

### Days 28–30: review economics and decide what to build next

Create a cohort report for every paid work order. Include revenue, professional payout, review minutes, support minutes, payment fees, rework, disputes, acceptance, time to completion, and buyer feedback.

Choose one of three decisions:

- **Scale the workflow:** repeat demand and positive contribution margin;
- **Refine the workflow:** buyer demand exists but quality or review cost is weak;
- **Stop the workflow:** no repeat demand or economics cannot work.

Only after this decision should the engineering roadmap prioritize the next job family.

## Weekly operating cadence

Every morning, review the funnel and next actions. Every afternoon, complete sales touches or pilot operations. Every Friday, review pipeline, delivery quality, support load, and contribution margin.

The founder should personally conduct early discovery and closing. Do not delegate the core sales learning before the buyer language and qualification criteria are stable.

## Objection handling

### “We already use Upwork or local freelancers.”

Agree. Position YayaAiki around the missing layer: structured acceptance, evidence, rework, provider-confirmed payment state, and a reusable assurance record. Offer to run one workflow where failed delivery or auditability is expensive.

### “We need a free trial.”

Offer a small, sharply scoped paid batch instead. Credit the design fee against production where useful, but do not absorb unlimited execution risk.

### “We cannot share sensitive data.”

Start with D0/D1 data, use redacted or synthetic samples for workflow design, define access roles, and document retention. Do not promise compliance beyond the actual controls and agreements.

### “We need pan-African capacity now.”

Explain that the pilot starts in Nigeria so acceptance, payout, and support can be measured. Offer expansion only after the first workflow passes economic and quality gates.

### “Can AI make the verification decision?”

No. AI may assist classification, missing-evidence detection, anomaly flags, or summaries. A human or explicit rule remains accountable for consequential decisions during the pilot.

## Success gates at day 30

The pilot is promising if it achieves all of the following:

- three to five paying anchor customers or signed paid pilot orders;
- at least three completed paid work orders or batches;
- at least two customers commit to repeat work;
- acceptance criteria can be written before work starts;
- evidence can be collected and reviewed consistently;
- payment state is confirmed through the provider or clearly reconciled;
- contribution margin is positive or has a specific measured path to positive;
- no unresolved high-severity privacy, payment, or access incident;
- the team can identify which workflow to scale and which to stop.

Do not treat the following as success by themselves: social impressions, professional signups, waitlist size, website traffic, investor meetings, press mentions, or a large number of tiny unpaid tasks.

## Metrics to keep from the first order

Track **Verified Economic Work Volume** across work orders, accepted deliverables, buyer revenue, professional payout, reviewer minutes, rework, disputes, contribution margin, and repeat orders.

Track **Repeat Assured Work** as the leading indicator:

> A buyer orders again after a delivered work order has been evidenced, reviewed, accepted, and paid.

This metric is more valuable than raw registrations and more difficult to manufacture.

## What not to build during these 30 days

Do not build USSD, voice, a public reputation score, a wallet, a payment processor, a generic talent directory, a large AI matching layer, cryptocurrency, a pan-African launch, or a microservice platform. Build only what makes the first paid work order reliable, auditable, and repeatable.

At the end of each qualified discovery call, ask directly:

> **Can we run one paid batch?**

Immediately after a successful delivery, ask:

> **Would you like us to run the next batch using the same workflow?**

A second paid order is stronger evidence than a subscription expression of interest. Convert repeat demand into a monthly workflow, minimum volume, delivery SLA, agreed pricing, and repeat-order commitment where appropriate.


## Strategic refinement: sell the action, not the platform

The primary conversion should be **Start a Work Order**, not “Book a Demo,” “Join the marketplace,” or “Explore the infrastructure.” The website should let a Nigerian business submit a real repeatable-work requirement asynchronously. Operations can remain human-assisted behind the interface while the customer experiences a clear productized workflow.

### Primary and secondary calls to action

- Primary: **Start a Work Order**
- Secondary: **See How It Works**
- Professional path: **Find Verified Work**
- Education path: **Explore YayaAiki**
- Support path: **Talk to YayaAiki**

The core message is:

> **Have work that needs doing? Start a Work Order. Give us the work. We organize the delivery.**

### Async-first funnel

`Content or outreach` → `Website` → `Start a Work Order` → `Work intake` → `Structured Work Order` → `YayaAiki review` → `Price/scope confirmation` → `Buyer accepts` → `Execution` → `Evidence` → `Verification` → `Buyer acceptance` → `Payment` → `Assurance Record` → `Repeat work`.

A call remains available as an escalation path, but it should not be required to submit the first real requirement.

### Channel mix

Prioritize channels according to Nigerian discovery and trust behavior:

1. WhatsApp for direct click-to-chat and referral conversion.
2. Facebook for problem-led posts and relevant business communities.
3. Instagram for visual process explainers and short carousels.
4. TikTok for practical videos about outsourcing failure, evidence, and acceptance.
5. Email for formal organizations and documented workflow proposals.
6. LinkedIn selectively for higher-value operations, data, research, procurement, and BPO contacts.

Every channel should send traffic to the real action. Do not use generic corporate announcements as the primary campaign format.

### Campaign content themes

Use concrete problem education instead of broad African-talent motivation:

- “What exactly was requested, delivered, checked, accepted, and paid?”
- “The difference between finding a freelancer and receiving accepted work.”
- “Why recurring outsourced work creates rework and payment disputes.”
- “How acceptance criteria make a work order safer to repeat.”
- “What a Work Assurance Record contains after completion.”

All examples must be labelled as illustrative until backed by real customer data.

### Revised Days 6–10 targets

- 100 targeted Nigerian organizations;
- 25–40 meaningful decision-maker interactions;
- 50–100 targeted interactions across WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok;
- targeted email and LinkedIn outreach;
- at least 10 real work requests entering the public intake funnel;
- at least 5 qualified opportunities;
- 3–5 paid pilots across the full 30-day period.

Track movement through the funnel rather than measuring views alone: landing visits, **Start a Work Order** clicks, intake starts, completed submissions, categories, volumes, deadlines, budgets, data classes, quotes, accepted quotes, paid pilots, completed work, buyer acceptance, and repeat requests.

### Website gate before scaling outreach

Before increasing outreach volume, test that an unauthenticated visitor can understand YayaAiki, start a work order, describe the expected output, define acceptance requirements, classify data sensitivity, submit, receive a public intake ID, and track the next status without speaking to the founder. If this path fails, fix the website and intake funnel before buying reach.
